Everyone knows what they should eat on a weeknight. The failure point is never knowledge — it is 8:40 pm, nothing is soaked, and the fastest thing in the house is a packet of something.

So this is not a menu. It is the shelf that makes a fast high-protein meal possible, and three shapes that cover almost every night.

## The shelf

Keep these in the house and a fifteen-minute meal stops being a decision:

- **Paneer.** Keeps for a week, needs no cooking to be safe to eat, and carries 18 to 20 g of protein per 100 g.
- **Eggs.** Six grams each, and the fastest protein there is.
- **Curd, or hung curd.** Five to six grams per katori, and the base of most cold bowls.
- **Soya chunks.** Around 15 g of protein per 30 g dry — the cheapest protein in the kitchen. They need about ten minutes in hot water first, which is why they belong on a shelf you plan with rather than a shelf you improvise from.
- **Cooked chana or rajma.** Not from a tin at 8:40 pm — from a batch you cooked on the weekend, in the fridge, ready. This is the difference between a fast meal and a takeaway.

Add whatever vegetable is in season, a lemon, and your masala box, and the arithmetic works out.

## Three shapes, not a hundred recipes

Almost every fast protein meal is one of these:

1. **The pocket.** Something stuffed into a roti, a dosa or a paratha, cooked on a tawa. One pan, no serving dish.
2. **The bowl.** Curd, oats, chana, sprouts, fruit, nuts, a squeeze of lemon. Zero to five minutes of work, and the combination is forgiving.
3. **The quick fry.** A hot pan, a protein, some vegetables, a finished masala. Ten minutes, and it doubles as lunch the next day.

Once you see meals in those three shapes, you stop needing recipes to be invented for every ingredient, and you start substituting.

Some nights there is no fifteen minutes. On those nights the honest answer is not a recipe, it is a bowl: curd or hung curd, something with texture, fruit, and something with fat in it so it holds you.

## Cook once, so tonight is fast

Every fast meal above depends on one thing having been done earlier. Not meal prep in the twelve-boxes-on-Sunday sense — just one pot.

Boil a batch of chana or rajma, soak soya chunks in bulk, roast a tray of vegetables, keep a bowl of sprouts growing. Twenty minutes on a weekend buys four weeknights, and it is the only part of this post that requires any planning at all. If you do one thing after reading this, do that one.

## The fifteen-minute habits

- **One pan.** Washing up is the reason people order in, not cooking.
- **Get the pan hot before anything else.** The technique in [the less-oil post](/blog/cooking-with-less-oil-tadka) is also a speed technique: a hot pan and a splash of water does the work oil usually does.
- **Prep while something cooks.** Chop the vegetables for tomorrow while the chana boils tonight.
- **Keep the protein visible.** In the fridge at eye level, not behind the vegetables. You eat what you see first — which is the whole argument for not keeping biscuits at eye level either.

## What these meals have in common

All three shapes put a deliberate protein source in the middle and build around it, which is exactly the habit in [how much protein you actually need](/blog/how-much-protein-do-you-need-indian-diet). Get to 30 to 40 g at a meal without spending 700 calories or an hour, and a good day stops being a rare event.
